# Story direction

Use this reference for a new page or a substantial redesign. For a small repair,
preserve the existing narrative and change only the affected beat.

## Find the spine

Reduce the brief to four decisions:

- **Subject:** the concrete product, place, process, or body of work.
- **Visitor:** who arrives and what they already know.
- **Change:** what becomes newly visible or understandable.
- **Action:** the useful next step after the story.

Choose a recurring visual motif that belongs to the subject. Examples include an
assembly separating into functional layers, a route connecting real locations, a
publication revealing its spreads, or a material changing under light. Avoid using
an abstract orb as a substitute for product thinking.

## Beat sheet

Keep one row per meaningful change, not per animation:

| Beat | Visitor learns | Focal material | Start → change → hold → exit | Mobile/static proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orientation | What this is and why it matters | Product and title | Readable opening → restrained reveal → stable frame → flow | Top frame |
| Signature | The central advantage or idea | Real mechanism or comparison | Establish → transform → labels settle → release | Before/after state |
| Evidence | Why the message is credible | Detail, work, demonstration, or source | Calm reading with optional local reveal | Midpoint |
| Action | What the visitor can do next | Existing route, form, or contact | Quiet landing without delay | Bottom frame |

The table is a thinking aid, not a required four-section layout. Remove any beat
that does not carry real content.

## Compose the interval

A useful starting shape for one scrubbed reveal is:

- establish during the first fifth;
- transform through the middle;
- hold the resolved state long enough to read;
- release cleanly into normal flow.

Inspect the midpoint. Two partly visible scenes are not automatically an intentional
transition. Keep copy still during its reading interval and let the visual layer
carry the transformation.

## Assets and identity

For every important asset record its source, purpose, focal point, intended crop,
loading priority, and fallback. Prefer supplied assets. Keep navigation and factual
claims as real text. When generating imagery is within scope, keep lighting,
material, camera language, and negative space consistent across the sequence.

Complete one vertical slice before expanding: readable opening, signature change,
release, and mobile/static equivalent. This reveals concept and geometry problems
while they are still cheap to fix.
